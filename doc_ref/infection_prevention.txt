WHY HAND HYGIENE MATTERS
Many illnesses spread when hands are not washed with soap and clean running water. Handwashing helps prevent respiratory infections and diarrheal infections. Germs move from person to person, or from surfaces to people, when someone:
- Touches their eyes, nose or mouth with unwashed hands
- Prepares or eats food or drinks with unwashed hands
- Touches surfaces or objects that carry germs
- Blows their nose, coughs or sneezes into their hands and then touches other people's hands or shared objects

KEY TIMES TO WASH HANDS
- Before, during and after preparing food
- Before and after eating
- Before and after caring for someone at home who has vomiting or diarrhea
- Before and after treating a cut or wound
- After using the toilet
- After changing diapers or cleaning a child who used the toilet
- After blowing your nose, coughing or sneezing
- After touching an animal, animal feed or animal waste
- After handling pet food or pet treats
- After touching garbage

HOW TO WASH HANDS (FIVE STEPS)
1. Wet hands with clean running water (warm or cold), turn off the tap, and apply soap.
2. Lather by rubbing hands together, including backs of hands, between fingers and under nails.
3. Scrub for at least 20 seconds (about the time it takes to hum "Happy Birthday" twice).
4. Rinse well under clean running water.
5. Dry with a clean towel or an air dryer.

WHEN SOAP AND WATER ARE NOT AVAILABLE
Soap and water is the best choice in most situations. If it is not readily available, use an alcohol-based hand sanitizer with at least 60% alcohol (check the product label).

RESPIRATORY ETIQUETTE (ONLY WHAT THE SOURCE SUPPORTS)
Coughing, sneezing or blowing your nose into your hands and then touching others or shared objects can spread germs, so washing hands after blowing your nose, coughing or sneezing is recommended.
